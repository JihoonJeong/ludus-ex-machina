# Chemical Hazard Reference — Laboratory Safety Manual
**Hanyang University, Department of Chemistry**
**Document:** Chem-Hazard-Ref-041
**Compound:** Sodium Azide (NaN₃)
**Revision:** March 2025

---

## Identification

- **CAS Number:** 26628-22-8
- **Molecular Formula:** NaN₃
- **Molecular Weight:** 65.01 g/mol
- **Appearance:** White crystalline powder
- **Odor:** Odorless (solid form)
- **Solubility:** Freely soluble in water
- **Hazard Classification:** Acute Toxicity Category 1 (Oral, Inhalation); Reactive — Acid-Sensitive

---

## Critical Physical Similarity Warning

**Sodium azide (NaN₃) is visually indistinguishable from sodium chloride (NaCl).** Both are white crystalline powders of similar particle size and density. This similarity has been implicated in laboratory accidents worldwide. All sodium azide containers must be labeled in **RED lettering** per University SOP and stored separately from salts and buffers.

---

## Acute Hazard — Reaction with Acids

Sodium azide reacts rapidly and exothermically with strong acids to produce **hydrazoic acid (HN₃)**, a colorless, volatile, and extraordinarily toxic gas:

> **NaN₃ + HCl → HN₃↑ + NaCl**

This reaction occurs at room temperature and proceeds quickly. The gas is released as bubbles from the reaction mixture and volatilizes immediately.

**Hydrazoic acid (HN₃) properties:**
- Colorless gas; pungent odor at high concentrations (may not be detectable at immediately dangerous levels)
- Vapor density: 1.45 (heavier than air — accumulates at bench level)
- **LC₅₀ (rat, inhalation, 4h):** 60 ppm
- **Estimated human lethal concentration:** 10–50 ppm over 1 hour exposure
- **IDLH (immediately dangerous to life or health):** 10 ppm
- Mechanism of toxicity: Inhibits cytochrome c oxidase (similar to cyanide), causing cellular asphyxia; also directly damages pulmonary tissue

**Gas generation estimate:** 10 g NaN₃ reacting with excess HCl generates approximately 3.7 L HN₃ gas at 25°C. In a sealed 40 m³ laboratory, this yields a concentration of approximately **93 ppm** — well above the lethal threshold, and nearly 10× the IDLH.

---

## Storage Requirements

- Store in clearly labeled RED containers in the dedicated azide cabinet (not with general reagents or salts).
- Do NOT store in proximity to acids.
- Never decant into unlabeled or mislabeled secondary containers.

---

## Emergency Response

In the event of suspected azide exposure: evacuate immediately, call 119, do not re-enter without SCBA respiratory protection. Provide toxicology report to EMS — standard opioid antidotes are ineffective; hydroxocobalamin may be used as supportive treatment.
