## Findings

### Azide Ion (N₃⁻) — POSITIVE

- **Peripheral blood concentration:** 85 μg/mL
- **Reference range (unexposed individuals):** < 0.1 μg/mL
- **Established lethal threshold (blood):** ≥ 20 μg/mL
- Azide ion also confirmed in lung tissue at 140 μg/g (wet weight).

### Hydrazoic Acid (HN₃) Metabolite Profile

The azide ion distribution pattern — highest concentration in lung tissue, with a gradient from cardiac to peripheral blood — is consistent with **inhalation** as the primary route of exposure. This pattern is inconsistent with oral ingestion (which typically shows higher gastrointestinal and hepatic azide concentrations) or dermal absorption.

No azide detected in stomach contents. No azide detected in vitreous humor (a negative indicator for systemic oral poisoning).

### Standard Toxicology Screen — NEGATIVE

Ethanol, opioids, benzodiazepines, barbiturates, stimulants, common prescription medications: all not detected.

---

## Interpretation

**Cause of death is acute hydrazoic acid (HN₃) gas inhalation.**

Hydrazoic acid (HN₃) is a colorless, volatile, and highly toxic gas produced when **sodium azide (NaN₃) reacts with strong acids** — most commonly hydrochloric acid (HCl):

> NaN₃ + HCl → **HN₃↑** + NaCl

HN₃ gas causes rapid, severe irritation of the respiratory mucosa, followed by pulmonary edema and respiratory failure. The decedent's blood azide concentration of 85 μg/mL significantly exceeds the established lethal threshold.

The inhalation-consistent distribution of azide and the severe pulmonary edema observed at autopsy are fully compatible with this mechanism.

**Recommendation:** Investigators should identify potential sources of sodium azide in Laboratory 307 and determine whether any acid was used in proximity to an azide compound at or near the estimated time of death.
