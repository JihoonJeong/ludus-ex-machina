# Autopsy Report

**Pathologist:** Dr. Shin Minjung, National Institute of Scientific Investigation, Seoul Regional Office
**Location of Death:** Hanyang University, Chemistry Building 3, Lab 307

## External Examination

The decedent is a well-nourished female appearing consistent with stated age of 27. Pronounced cyanosis of the lips and fingernail beds. Bilateral petechial hemorrhages observed in the conjunctivae. Frothy discharge noted at the mouth and nares. No external traumatic injuries. No signs of restraint or struggle. Clothing (lab coat, jeans, long-sleeve shirt) intact and undisturbed. Gloves were found on the bench adjacent to the body, not worn at time of death.

## Internal Examination

**Respiratory System:** Severe bilateral pulmonary edema. Lung weight: Right 680g (normal ~450g), Left 620g (normal ~400g). Tracheobronchial mucosa shows diffuse erythema and superficial erosion consistent with acute chemical irritant exposure. Frothy fluid in the bronchioles.

**Cardiovascular System:** Mild right ventricular dilation consistent with acute cor pulmonale secondary to respiratory failure. No coronary artery disease or structural abnormality.

**Central Nervous System:** Mild cerebral edema. No hemorrhage, infarct, or structural lesion identified.

**Other Systems:** Unremarkable. No internal injuries identified.

## Cause and Manner of Death

**Immediate Cause:** Acute hypoxic respiratory failure
**Underlying Cause:** Acute toxic chemical inhalation (agent to be confirmed by toxicology)
**Manner of Death:** Homicide (pending full investigation)

## Estimated Time of Death

Based on body temperature (core: 30.1°C at examination at 14:00), liver mortis pattern, and scene conditions (ambient temperature 21°C), estimated time of death is **03:00–05:00 AM, April 2, 2026**.

## Toxicology Samples Submitted

Blood (cardiac and peripheral), vitreous humor, urine, and lung tissue submitted to toxicology laboratory. Results pending. See separate toxicology report.

## Conclusions

Findings are consistent with death caused by inhalation of a toxic chemical agent at the scene. The absence of trauma and intact clothing argue strongly against physical assault. The pulmonary findings are consistent with a reactive gas injury to the airway.
