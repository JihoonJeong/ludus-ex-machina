# Autopsy Report — Victim: Ms. Hwang Eunyoung

**National Forensic Service, Case #2026-NFS-0325-G**
**Date of Autopsy:** March 26, 2026

## Cause of Death
Cardiac arrest secondary to **acute hyperkalemia**. Blood potassium level at time of death: **9.2 mEq/L** (normal range: 3.5–5.0 mEq/L). This level is incompatible with sustained cardiac function and is consistent with exogenous potassium chloride administration.

## Toxicology Results
- **Blood potassium:** 9.2 mEq/L (critically elevated)
- **Midazolam:** Detected at therapeutic levels (consistent with prescribed 2mg IV dose)
- **Botulinum toxin:** Trace levels at injection sites (normal, cosmetic doses)
- **IV bag residue analysis:** **Potassium chloride (KCl) detected at approximately 40 mEq concentration.** Normal saline should contain 0 mEq KCl. This concentration would cause fatal hyperkalemia within 15-25 minutes of IV infusion.
- **Alcohol/recreational drugs:** None detected
- **Losartan, Atorvastatin:** Detected at expected therapeutic levels

## Physical Examination
- No signs of struggle, defensive wounds, or restraint marks
- IV insertion site: left antecubital fossa, clean insertion, no extravasation
- Botox injection sites: consistent with standard cosmetic application
- No signs of anaphylaxis (no edema, no urticaria)
- Heart: no pre-existing structural abnormalities, no evidence of prior infarction
- All organs: age-appropriate, no significant pathology

## Forensic Analysis of IV Bag
- The IV bag was a standard 500mL normal saline bag (manufacturer: JW Pharmaceutical)
- The bag's injection port showed **a single additional puncture mark** consistent with a syringe needle insertion
- The puncture was through the medication port (the same port used to add midazolam)
- **It is impossible to determine whether the KCl was added before or after the midazolam** from physical examination alone, as both would use the same port

## Expert Opinion
The concentration of KCl found in the IV bag (approximately 40 mEq in 500mL) is far in excess of any therapeutic application. In a clinical setting, IV potassium is administered at a maximum rate of 10 mEq/hour with continuous cardiac monitoring. The concentration found would have delivered a lethal dose within minutes of infusion start.

While accidental KCl contamination during IV manufacturing has been documented in rare cases (3 incidents reported in South Korea since 2020), the concentration found here (40 mEq) significantly exceeds levels seen in contamination events (typically <5 mEq). The forensic team considers deliberate addition the most likely explanation, though manufacturing error cannot be entirely ruled out without examining the supply chain.

## Note on Potassium Chloride
KCl is readily available in medical settings (used for electrolyte replacement), pharmacies, and chemical supply companies. In sufficient concentration, it causes cardiac arrest that can initially mimic natural cardiac death. Without the detailed toxicology screen, this death might have been attributed to a pre-existing cardiac condition or procedure-related complication.
