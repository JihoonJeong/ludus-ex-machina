# Fire Investigation Report — Art Space Pado

**Source:** Busan Fire Investigation Division
**Report No.:** BFD-2026-0320-A14
**Date:** March 21, 2026

## Fire Summary

| Item | Details |
|------|---------|
| Location | Art Space Pado, 42 Haeundae-ro, Haeundae-gu, Busan |
| Date/Time | March 20, 2026, approx. 03:00 AM |
| Duration | ~45 minutes before suppression |
| Damage | ~80% of exhibited artworks destroyed; structural damage to interior |

## Cause Determination: **Intentional Arson**

### Accelerant

- **Turpentine (테레빈유)** detected at two separate ignition points
- Ignition Point 1: Main exhibition hall, northeast corner (near installation art section)
- Ignition Point 2: Storage room at rear of gallery
- Estimated volume of accelerant used: 3-5 liters total

### Ignition Method

- No timing device or electrical ignition mechanism found
- Burn pattern indicates **direct manual ignition** — the perpetrator was physically present at the time of ignition
- Time between ignition of Point 1 and Point 2 estimated at 2-4 minutes (based on burn progression analysis)

### Fire Suppression

- Automatic sprinkler system was **disabled** — control valve found in the OFF position
- The valve is located in the utility room accessible via the building's service corridor
- Gallery owner confirmed the sprinkler system was operational during the last inspection (February 15, 2026)

## Missing Artworks

Three artworks were removed from the gallery **prior to the fire**:
- Empty frames remained on the exhibition walls
- Frame mounting hardware was intact (paintings were carefully removed, not torn or cut)
- The missing works are detailed in: `missing_artworks.md`

## Entry Point

- No signs of forced entry at any exterior door or window
- The gallery's security card access system recorded one entry during the relevant timeframe (see: `security_card_log.md`)
- A service corridor connects the gallery to the adjacent building's parking structure; this corridor has no security camera and no card reader (key lock only)

## Conclusion

The fire was intentionally set using turpentine at two locations, with the sprinkler system manually disabled. The perpetrator had physical access to the gallery, knowledge of the sprinkler system location, and the ability to remove artworks carefully before starting the fire. This suggests someone familiar with the gallery's layout and operations.
